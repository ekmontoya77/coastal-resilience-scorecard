Who We Are

Building coastal resilience for a climate-accelerated world

Coastal cities and their key stakeholders face growing, debilitating uncertainty that undermines resilience and sustainability performance. The Scorecard equips public agencies, private sector leaders, and communities with a practitioner-led framework for enhancing prevention, response and recovery capabilities for coastal zone settlements.


ARISE · Private Sector Alliance for Disaster Resilient Societies

Aligned to the 10 Essentials for Making Cities Resilient by 2030 with direct oversight and support from ARISE, the private sector alliance arm of the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR) — the Scorecard combines institutional credibility, global community of practice, and proven resilience methodologies to coastal disaster risk reduction.

World Ocean Council (Barcelona) · Centre of Excellence for Coastal Resilience

This global, open-source initiative is nested within the World Ocean Council Centre of Excellence for Coastal Resilience in Barcelona — bringing together ocean industry leadership, institutional capital and philanthropy, and global networks in service of coastal communities worldwide.

Our Mission
To accelerate coastal resilience strategy development by connecting stakeholders with evidence-based tools, peer networks, blue finance pathways, and cross-sector capacity building.
The Challenge
From episodic hazard to deep uncertainty — cities now navigate non-linear, compound coastal risks with no probability ceiling, undermining planning across every time horizon.
The Response
A multi-generational, multi-sequence strategy spanning prevention, response, and recovery — adapting greenfield and brownfield assets, sites, and coastlines to stabilise communities and economies.

Theory of Change

Why coastal resilience cannot wait

Three global conditions are compounding one another, and none of them can be solved in isolation. The Coastal Resilience Scorecard sits where all three intersect — turning that compounding into a single evidence base that cities and companies can act on.

🌊

Accelerating & Compounding Hazards

Natural and human-induced disasters and chronic stresses are compounding one another — exceeding the design tolerances built into coastal zone assets and their planning frameworks.

🔴

Natural & Engineered Physical Gaps

Ecosystem decay meets unsuitable built environment design, construction, operations and maintenance — degrading natural buffers while engineered assets underperform against conditions they were not designed to face.

🏙️

Governance & Financing Gaps

Most cities lack integrated coastal resilience strategies that span idiosyncratic asset-level risks and the systemic, ecosystem-level context within which those assets sit.

The Programme

Blue & Resilience Finance as the cross-cutting container across three phases of coastal disaster risk reduction

Our programme equips stakeholders with structured tools, peer learning, and coordinated implementation development anchored to emerging standards and guidance for innovating the funding and financing of natural and engineered resilience solutions.

Cross-Cutting

Blue & Resilience Finance

Essential 3 · Strengthen Financial Capacity for Resilience

Monetising resilience across the full investment lifecycle.

Pre-investment

Scorecard gap analysis is translated into bankable project definition, use-of-proceeds design, and capital structuring.

At execution and ongoing performance

Resilient design earns cost savings through risk transfer — impacting cost of capital and on-the-run performance enhancements.

Post-investment

It earns again — as operating profit across the life of the asset, and as sale-on value at exit — closing the loop between the resilience premium and the cost of capital.

Anticipate, Restore & Harden
